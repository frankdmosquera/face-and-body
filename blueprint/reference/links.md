# Design links

One line per link. Say what to look at, not just where it is.

A bare URL is close to useless six weeks later. `https://example.com` tells you
nothing; "the way the pricing cards stack on mobile" tells you why you saved it.

## Format

```
- <url> - what to look at, and why
```

## Design references, 2026-09-17

Full-page screenshots sit next to this file as `inspo-*.png`. Screenshots freeze
the first frame, so the video heroes read as stills. Motion notes are here.

- https://www.skinspirit.com - `inspo-skinspirit.png`. Cream ground, serif caps
  headline with a thin underline, row of tall portrait crops for categories,
  dark green testimonial band with three quote cards. Closest to our direction.
- https://everbody.com - `inspo-everbody.png`. Warm beige, serif headlines.
  Concern-based navigation ("I'm interested in treatments for: fine lines,
  pigmentation, texture, hydration, acne"). Before and after cards with
  concern tags. Free consultation block with three bullets. FAQ accordion.
  The best clinic page structure of the six.
- https://peachystudio.com - `inspo-peachy.png`. Muted video hero of a
  treatment, three-icon trust strip under it, giant wordmark in the footer.
  Video is a slow pan, no cuts.
- https://formulafig.com - `inspo-formulafig.png`. Full-bleed photography,
  one brand colour saturating every image. Hero is a video of the treatment
  room. Depends entirely on photography quality.
- https://flawlessskincenter.com - `inspo-flawless.png`. Warm sand and copper
  watercolour bands. Proof the palette works for a clinic. Most of the page
  lazy-loads so the capture is thin.
- https://minimaleskin.com - not kept. Lazy-loaded to a blank capture.

## Placeholder photography

The mockups in `prototypes/` hotlink Pexels photos by ID
(`images.pexels.com/photos/<id>/...`) so the layout can be judged with real
photography in it. They are stand-ins for her own photos, chosen by slot type
(facial, massage, skincare, portrait, interior, clinic, orchid), not by
content. None of them ship. LoremFlickr was tried first and rejected: its
tag matching returns statues and race cars for "facial".
